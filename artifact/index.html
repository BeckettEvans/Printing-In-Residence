    What this guide is and who it is for

    Printing in Residence is a guide for UFV students who live in student housing and own, or are about to buy, a Bambu Lab H2S 3D printer. It covers the three things other guides leave out: asking Housing first, getting the printer online, and setting up your room so it passes inspection.

    You do not need any 3D printing or networking background. If you can use a phone and a laptop, you can follow every step.

    Not an official UFV document

    Three CMNS 390 students made this guide. It does not come from UFV Housing or from Bambu Lab.

    The 2026-27 housing handbook does not mention 3D printers at all (University of the Fraser Valley [UFV], 2026). That means Housing's written answer to you always comes before anything on this site. If you are not sure about something, email housing@ufv.ca and ask.

    Pick your building

    The internet works differently in each building, so the first step is knowing which one you live in.

    • H
      H Building (Lá:lem te Baker) Get Online in H Building
    • J
      J Building (Lá:léms Ye EverGreen) Get Online in J Building

    H Building (Lá:lem te Baker). You live in a Double, with two private bedrooms that share a kitchenette and a bathroom. Your bedroom has its own Wi-Fi modem on hardwired Rogers internet (UFV, 2026). Follow Get Online in H Building.

    J Building (Lá:léms Ye EverGreen). You live in a Quad, a Studio or a Single. The building uses eduroam, and devices that cannot join eduroam go on a second network called UFV-Gamer (UFV, 2026). Follow Get Online in J Building.

    Every other page is the same for both buildings. H Building is closed for construction right now, so we tested every step in J Building only.

    Before you start checklist

    Have these ready before you go any further:

    • You know which building you live in.
    • You have Housing's answer in writing. If you do not, start with Ask Housing First.
    • You know the printer's numbers. It weighs 30 kg, it is about 49 cm wide, 51 cm deep and 63 cm tall, and it can draw up to 1,170 W for about 3 minutes while the bed heats up (Bambu Lab, 2025).
    • You have a Bambu Lab account and the Bambu Handy app on your phone.
    • You have your UFV student email and password.
    • J Building only: you know where to find the printer's MAC address. We show you on the J Building page.
    • You are not planning to use your own Wi-Fi router. Routers are on the prohibited items list (UFV, 2026).

    The five steps at a glance

    The whole guide is five steps. Each one has its own page.

    1. Find your building. You just did this above.
    2. Ask Housing first. Email Housing before the printer arrives and keep their reply.
    3. Get online. Follow the page for H Building or J Building.
    4. Fix connection problems. Check the troubleshooting table if the printer will not connect.
    5. Set up your room. Pick the spot, sort out power and air, and get ready for inspection.

    Safety note

    Safety

    The H2S is a machine that gets hot. The bed and nozzle heat up, and the chamber can reach 65 °C (Bambu Lab, 2025). It also pulls its full power for about 3 minutes at the start of a print.

    The printer has a HEPA filter and an activated carbon filter built in. That helps with air quality, but it does not replace asking Housing.

    Room Setup and Inspection covers power, air, noise and fire safety in detail. Read it before your first print in your room.

    Guide / Reference / Glossary

    Glossary

    Draft. Beckett's terms from the Master Draft; Jade's and Bennett's terms still to come.

    Terms

    Bambu Handy
    Bambu Lab's phone app. You use it to set up the printer, watch a print and control it from your phone.
    Bambu Studio
    Bambu Lab's free computer program. It turns a 3D model into a file the printer can print, which is called slicing.
    Community Assistant (CA)
    A student staff member who lives in residence and supports the residents on their floor.
    eduroam
    The secure Wi-Fi network used at universities. You sign in with your student email and password.
    H Building
    Lá:lem te Baker, the UFV residence with two-bedroom Double suites.
    J Building
    Lá:léms Ye EverGreen, the six-storey UFV residence with Quads, Studios and Singles.
    MAC address
    A code built into every device that connects to a network. It is six pairs of letters and numbers separated by colons.
    UFV-Gamer
    A second network in J Building for devices that cannot sign in to eduroam. Housing adds your device by its MAC address (UFV, 2026).

    Printer explorer

    Extra page. Not one of the nine guide pages; remove this file and its menu link to leave it out.

    Look around the printer

    Drag to turn the printer, pinch or scroll to zoom, and pick a part to see what it does. This is a simplified illustration, not to scale, and part positions are approximate. For the real thing, use Bambu Lab's H2S introduction (Bambu Lab, n.d.-e).

    Parts

    • The body of the printer. Everything else is mounted to it, and the door and cover close it in.

    • Closes the top of the enclosure. The printer detects when it is open.

    • Closes the front of the enclosure. The printer detects when it is open.

    • Two motors and two belts move the tool head left, right, forward and back.

    • Carries the extruder, the hotend and its single nozzle, plus fans and sensors.

    • The heated print surface. A textured build plate attaches to it with magnets and flexes to release prints.

    • Three lead screws raise and lower the heatbed evenly.

    • The 5-inch screen you use to control the printer. The Wi-Fi settings are found here.

    • Plugs into the back of the printer. It must be inserted for the printer to power on.
